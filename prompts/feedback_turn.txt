STEP 1. L1 — Impact on meaning (1–10): Can the listener fully understand the speaker's intended meaning?

(1) literal_ko — Listener's understanding: Imagine a native English speaker who does not know Korean hears the sentence exactly as spoken. Translate into Korean what they would understand, WITHOUT fixing errors. Write only the single most likely interpretation. If it cannot be understood, write "이해 불가".
    other_interpretation — Is another interpretation also possible? (true/false)
    needs_effort — Would a typical listener actually have to stop, think again, or ask back to understand it? (true/false)
                   Noticing an error is NOT effort. Mark true only if the meaning is not clear on first hearing.
(2) intended_ko — Speaker's intention: Using the conversation context, translate into Korean what the learner meant to say.
(3) Compare literal_ko and intended_ko (same_meaning: true/false), combine with the two check answers, and convert to a score.
    Score by the most serious problem that is clearly present; do not add up small problems into a higher band.

L1 scoring guide
1–2: Meaning comes across fully. Either no error, or only small slips that do not touch the meaning at all.
     e.g. "I went to a café with my friend." → listener: 친구와 카페에 갔다 / speaker: same
     e.g. "I went to cafe with my friends yesterday." (missing article) → same meaning, no effort
3–4: There is a noticeable error, but the listener still understands it right away, without stopping.
     e.g. "She go to school by bus." → listener: 그녀는 버스로 학교에 간다 / speaker: same
5–6: Understood as intended, but only with real effort: the listener has to pause, guess from context, or piece the words together.
     e.g. "Yesterday I go place my friend say good for eat." → listener: 어제 친구가 먹기 좋다고 한 곳에 갔다 (effort needed) / speaker: same
7–8: Two or more interpretations are genuinely plausible and the context does not settle which one is meant.
     e.g. "My sister and mom had a fight, and she was angry." → listener: 언니 또는 엄마가 화났다 (other interpretation possible) / speaker: 엄마가 화났다
9–10: The listener would most likely understand a different meaning from the intention, or cannot recover the meaning at all.
     e.g. "I'm boring." → listener: 나는 지루한 사람이다 / speaker: 나 지루해
     e.g. "My boss is very sensible, so I always check my words." → listener: 상사가 분별 있어서 말을 조심한다 / speaker: 상사가 예민해서 말을 조심한다

Sentence: "they just create impression such well that people are drag to buy it."

Judgment: same meaning, but understanding takes effort → 5–6

STEP 2. L2 — Naturalness of expression (1–10): How likely is it that a native speaker would say it this way in this conversation?

(1) minimal_revision — Make the sentence grammatical for SPOKEN English with the fewest possible changes.
(2) natural_revision — Revise only as much as needed so it sounds like a native speaker said it. Preserve the meaning; make no unnecessary changes.
(3) Compare the original with the two revisions, decide what kind of change was really needed, and convert to a score.
    A difference between the original and natural_revision does not by itself justify a high score: simple, plain or textbook wording is acceptable.

L2 scoring guide
1–2: Fine as it is. A native speaker could say it this way, or the only differences are optional style choices (spoken ellipsis, discourse markers and simple vocabulary are fine).
     e.g. "Went there yesterday. It was fun."
3–4: Acceptable and natural enough in conversation. A more idiomatic alternative exists, or there are small grammar slips a native speaker would simply let pass.
     e.g. "I'm very hungry." → "I'm starving."
     e.g. "I watched a movie with my family at last weekend."
5–6: Communication works, but the phrasing clearly sounds non-native; a native speaker would almost certainly put it another way.
     e.g. "I have a plan to go to Busan this weekend." → "I'm going to Busan this weekend."
7–8: Clearly awkward or wrong to a native speaker (a wrong collocation or construction that stands out), though still understandable.
     e.g. "make a photo" → "take a photo"
9–10: Pragmatically inappropriate for the situation (clearly more direct than needed, or rude).
     e.g. (when asking a favor) "Give me your notes."

Worked example
Sentence: "they just create impression such well that people are drag to buy it."
minimal_revision: "They just create an impression so well that people are dragged to buy it."
natural_revision: "They just create such a good impression that people are compelled to buy it."

Every change in revised must appear in items, and items must not mention changes that are not in revised.
